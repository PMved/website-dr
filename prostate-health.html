Anatomy & Signs

Understanding Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia (BPH)

The prostate is a walnut-sized gland surrounding the male urethra. With age, hormonal shifts cause the prostate to enlarge and squeeze the urinary pipe, producing characteristic urinary symptoms.

Frequent Night Urination (Nocturia)

Waking up 2, 3, or more times every night to pass urine, leading to fragmented sleep and fatigue. One of the earliest warning signs of an enlarged prostate.

Weak Stream & Hesitancy

Waiting several seconds for the urine stream to start, a thin or split flow, and straining to empty the bladder completely.

Urgency & Incomplete Emptying

A sudden, compelling urge to urinate that is difficult to postpone, accompanied by the persistent sensation that the bladder is never completely empty.

Early Detection

The PSA Blood Test & Prostate Cancer Awareness

Serum PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen) is a simple blood test that helps evaluate prostate health and detect potential abnormalities early.

Why Every Man Over 50 Should Know His PSA Level

Prostate cancer is often asymptomatic in its earliest, most curable stages. That is why routine screening with a Total PSA blood test and Digital Rectal Examination (DRE) is recommended starting at age 50 (or age 45 for men with a family history of prostate cancer).

An elevated PSA does NOT necessarily mean cancer. Common non-cancerous conditions such as BPH, urinary tract infections, prostatitis, or recent catheterization can also raise PSA levels. Dr. Chipde conducts detailed diagnostic assessments (Free-to-Total PSA ratio, multiparametric MRI prostate) to determine whether further investigation is needed.

PSA Reference Guidance (General Overview):

  • 0 to 2.5 ng/mL: Normal range for most younger men.
  • 2.5 to 4.0 ng/mL: Generally normal, evaluated based on age and prostate volume.
  • 4.0 to 10.0 ng/mL: "Borderline zone". Often caused by BPH or inflammation; clinical evaluation recommended.
  • Above 10.0 ng/mL: Warrants structured urological evaluation, multiparametric MRI, and possible targeted biopsy.

*PSA interpretation must be conducted by a qualified urologist in conjunction with clinical history and examination.
